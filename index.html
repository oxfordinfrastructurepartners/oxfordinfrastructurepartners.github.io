The Audit
Dear Bob,

Who we are

Oxford Infrastructure Partners researches, builds and finances the infrastructure powering the AI economy. Across three capabilities, Research, Commercial and Investment, we secure commercial frameworks for specialist technology partners. For Laser Light, that means one thing: acting as your outsourced commercial engine, building pipeline, winning contracts, and taking you from first customer conversation through to Financial Close.

How we found you

We have followed the public rollout of your Einstein-standard AI Corridor, your "Rise of the AI Corridor" panel at ITW, and the announcements around the Pennsylvania build. The architecture is serious, the leadership is unusually deep, and the timing is real. That combination is exactly where a structured commercial engine turns a strong story into signed revenue, which is why you stood out to us.

Audit · Step 1

Your offering

The strength and sellability of what you have built, mapped to how commercial engagements are priced.

A genuinely differentiated architecture

HALO traces back to the USAF Transformational Satellite program, and you have patented a model for secure optical data distribution across space, ground, and subsea. A defensible technical position, not a reseller story.

One integrated data utility, not a point product

Fiber, subsea, an optical-satellite layer, and AI orchestration through FastLight.AI as a single software-defined network. Positioned as a utility rather than a telco, it maps cleanly to Software and Services frameworks.

A concrete first build with a story

The Einstein-standard AI Corridor, starting in Pennsylvania, gives the offering what most infrastructure plays lack this early: a specific, physical, nameable first deployment buyers and communities can point to.

Scale already referenced publicly

You publicly reference up to $600M in contracted terrestrial and subsea capacity and 27+ global routes at ~140-month average duration. We treat these as your stated figures; they signal an offering that is already commercial, not theoretical.

Audit · Step 2

Your commercial position

We always start with what is genuinely working. Here, a great deal is.

Leadership that opens doors

Your track record runs through TerreStar Networks, Deutsche Telekom Americas, Bell Atlantic, and General Counsel of the U.S. Department of Commerce. Few founders in this sector can convene the rooms you can.

A real technical and strategic bench

Greg Zappala at Far Edge Solutions, a CTO and systems architect in Steve Birrell, edge and AI leadership in Tom Koster, and a Global AI Bureau under Vincent Perez. The build side is staffed by people who have done it before.

Credible anchors and partnerships

A sister-company relationship with CommStar into the cislunar domain, a supplier relationship with Mynaric, ITW panel presence, engagement around the DoE Genesis Mission, and outreach to CMU, Pitt, Drexel, and Penn. The ecosystem is forming.

A demand story the market now agrees with

Your thesis, that AI has outpaced the data-distribution layer and that data must move like a utility to the edge, is no longer contrarian. The market has caught up to where you have been for years.

Audit · Step 3

Your go-to-market signals

Based only on what is public. This is where the gap lives.

The outward motion is narrative and capital, not sales

What is visible is thought leadership, the film series, The Updraft, and a capital allocation for accredited investors. We see the story engine and the funding engine. We do not see a structured commercial engine converting route-users into signed capacity contracts.

No buyer-facing commercial package is visible

For a scale user, a carrier, a health system, or a university deciding to buy capacity, there is no visible one-pager, proposal set, or terms framework built to make that decision easy. The materials that exist are built to inspire, not to close.

Conversion appears founder-led, not systematized

From the outside, momentum tracks to your relationships and your calendar. That is powerful, and it does not scale. We see no visible, repeatable motion working a prioritised target-account list from first touch to signature.

The Problems

What those findings reveal

Now that we know what is there, here is what stands between you and the revenue this offering should be producing.

Problem 01

The engine is you, and you do not scale

You have built a defensible architecture and staffed the build. What is missing is the machine that turns route-users into signed customers without routing every deal through your own relationships. Based on what is public, the commercial motion today looks like this:

A panel or a post creates interestInterest arrives through your thought leadership, a conference, or an introduction.
The conversation waits for youMomentum depends on your presence, your network, and your time.
No standard package to hand overEach opportunity is bespoke, so nothing compounds and nothing repeats.
Follow-up is whoever is freeWithout a dedicated commercial owner, qualified interest cools between the panel and the paperwork.

An architecture built to serve a nation should not have a pipeline that fits in one person's calendar.

Problem 02

The revenue is anchored to the build, not to this quarter

Your public narrative points at a future: the corridor, the orbital layer, the Moon by 2028. That vision is a genuine asset. The risk is that near-term commercial conversion drifts behind it.

The build is fundable, the pipeline is undefined

You cite $600M in contracted capacity. What is not visible is the structured motion that adds the next contract, and the one after that, along the towns the corridor passes through.

Capital is being raised to fund growth

A Reg D allocation is open. Contracted revenue and raised capital are not substitutes. The strongest position is a raise supported by a visible, growing pipeline, not standing in place of one.

Demand you can measure is not demand you are converting

You have said the demand along the route is measurable. Measurable demand with no structured motion to convert it is revenue sitting in plain sight.

The market agreeing with your thesis is not the same as the market signing your contracts.

Problem 03

Built to inspire, not packaged to buy

Your public materials are excellent at making people believe. They are not built to make a buyer act. Those are two different jobs, and the second is where revenue is won.

What should be there: a commercial one-pager and proposal set that translate HALO, FastLight.AI, and the corridor into what a specific buyer gets, on what terms, at what price posture, with what onboarding. A prioritised target-account list. A repeatable outreach motion with a named owner. A defined path from first conversation to executed contract.

Belief moves audiences. Packaging moves procurement.
